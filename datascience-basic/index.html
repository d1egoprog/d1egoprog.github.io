The starting point

Good research starts
with understanding your data

This course brings together the foundations of data science and the practical work of data management. We begin with a research question, examine the data behind it, and explore how analysis can support a defensible interpretation.

You will work with data formats, cleaning decisions, introductory AI, and text analysis. Documentation, visualization, and responsible reuse run through the course.

What you will practice

  • Describe a dataset, its origin, and its limitations.
  • Recognize common formats and prepare data for analysis.
  • Explain the purpose of a model and how to evaluate it.
  • Communicate a simple analysis with a clear chart and reproducible steps.

Before you begin: bring basic computer skills, an interest in research, and a willingness to experiment. The practical work introduces Python and notebooks step by step.

The learning path

Course outline

Five topics, with a research activity for each. Open a topic to see what we will cover.

00Course introductionYour research question is the starting point.

Research context, expectations, participation

Connect data science to your doctoral project and discuss the data you already use or need to collect.

Research activity. Introduce your research question, the data it requires, and one challenge you would like to solve during the course.

Find the slides in resources ↗
01Data science foundationsHow observations become evidence.

The data science process, data sources, the Big Data Vs

Understand how domain knowledge, statistics, and computing work together. Discuss volume, variety, velocity, and the quality and value of data.

Research activity. Map a research project from its question to data collection, exploration, analysis, and interpretation. Identify where you might need to revisit an earlier step.

Find the slides in resources ↗
02A gentle introduction to AIThe basic ideas behind learning from data.

Descriptive and predictive analysis, machine learning, evaluation

Distinguish describing a dataset from predicting an outcome. Explore how models learn patterns and why evaluation on unseen data matters.

Research activity. Choose a possible prediction task in your field. Describe its inputs, expected output, a simple baseline, and how you would assess its results.

Find the slides in resources ↗
03Data, formats, and research reuseMake a dataset understandable to someone else.

Metadata, CSV / DSV, JSON, XML, ETL, licensing, FAIR

Recognize structured, semi-structured, and unstructured data. Explain extraction, transformation, and loading, and document data with metadata and reuse conditions.

Research activity. Inspect the file examples in the resource library. Prepare a small dataset with a data dictionary, a description of its origin, and a record of your cleaning decisions.

Find the slides in resources ↗
04Text as data with PythonA first analysis of a collection of documents.

Python, pandas, tokenization, stopwords, bag of words, charts

Follow a basic NLP workflow from a delimited text file to a frequency chart. Compare preprocessing choices and explain what a bag-of-words representation leaves out.

Research activity. Load a text column, inspect it, tokenize it, and count terms. Create a labelled chart and explain how your cleaning choices affect its interpretation.
